## Brief (step 2)

The video pitches selling five AI automations to small businesses, each with a setup fee and a monthly retainer. It says chatbots are saturated and these five "have no competition at all" (open). Stated prices: lead response bot, "1.5k to 3k" setup, "$400 to $800" monthly, "two to three days" to build (service 1); review generation, "1.5k to 3k", "300 to 600" monthly, "one to two days" (service 2); cold email machines, "3k to 6k", "1k to 2.5k" monthly, "five to seven days", for agencies, SaaS firms and consultants (service 3); AI receptionist, "2.5k to 5k", "497 to 1.2k" monthly plus "a dollar" per minute, "four to five days" (service 4); SOP knowledge bot, "5k to 10k", "1k to 2.5k" monthly, "five to seven days", for "50 to 500 employee companies", hardest to close (service 5). Examples: a gym goes "47 reviews" to "187" and gets "three times inbound leads in six weeks" (service 2); "30 to 40 %" of businesses lose revenue from after-hours calls (service 4). Advice: start with the lead response bot, "the easiest to build" (close). The video names no tools, no software costs and no way to find the first client. No result from the presenter's own clients is shown. It offers "a free school community" with scripts and templates (close).

## The three tells

- Does the video sell something: yes, a free one. A free school community with a sales script, templates and frameworks (close). No paid offer appears in the video or the description excerpt; paid tiers were not checked.
- Does it show costs beside results: no. Only build time in days appears. No tool, software, usage or ad cost, and no cost of finding the first client.
- Does it state a time frame: in part. Build times per service, "six weeks" in the gym example (row 10) and "week one" for the receptionist (row 11). No time frame for landing a first client or reaching an income figure.
