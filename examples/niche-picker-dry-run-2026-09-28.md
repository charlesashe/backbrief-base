## The filter

payers (a second one). Gap 4 stays in the scan and produces no candidate, because three other
gaps survived.

```
The panel's read: close the gaps first - price and proof that anyone pays are both missing.
First move: Report sketch - in the Saturday block, build one report he wishes his software gave him in a blank spreadsheet with placeholder numbers, list the fields it needs, and end with a one-sentence description a stranger could read. Moves Clarity of offer and Execution feasibility.

This is a stress-test, not a certification. A pass means no confident weakness was left un-named. A low score is the useful result.
```

Why these scores:
- Clarity of offer: the buyer is an office manager on a system like the two in Gap 1, and the product is templates for monthly totals. The brief names no specific report.
- Market realism: two posts on one review site call billing hard or reports slow (Capterra, 2025, verified as posted). No seeker thread was found and nothing shows anyone paying for a fix.
- Financial viability: price not stated. The operator's own figures, 6 hours a week and $800 (unverified), yield neither revenue nor a cost per sale.
- Execution feasibility: "spreadsheet builds" is a stated skill and fixed blocks suit a template build. He works in one system every day, so other systems' export formats are a learning gap.
- Risk coverage: whether summary exports fall outside health-privacy rules is an unverified local-law assumption (United States federal, Ohio not checked) and earns no credit. His employer's view of outside work in the field is an Open row.
- Differentiation: against doing nothing or the vendors' own report tools, the brief offers his practitioner experience, a claim with no outside corroboration.
- Reachability (trial): one Facebook group where he is a member and a LinkedIn network of former coworkers. No list, no site, no cold outreach. Whether the group allows selling is an Open row.
