# Business Brief: TrailNotes

Date: 2026-09-29. Produced by /intake from the owner's brain dump (inputs/trailnotes-brain-dump-2026-08-17.md, used verbatim). This is the single source the plan is built from; if it is wrong, fix it here first.

**Specimen disclosure:** TrailNotes is Backbrief's disclosed invented example business, not a real company. The dump states that its numbers are "the example's premise." Every figure below is the owner's own statement of what the owner's records say. Intake did not check any of them.

## The idea in one paragraph
TrailNotes is a $6/month email newsletter of current, on-the-ground hiking trail conditions for one metro area (mid-size, mountain-adjacent, about 1.2M people, about 90 maintained trailheads within an hour's drive). Every Thursday night subscribers get one email: which trails are clear, muddy, snowed in, washed out or closed, what parking looked like last weekend, and a short "if you only do one hike this weekend" pick. Conditions come from three sources: the owner's own weekend scouting (two trails every weekend), a private report form filled in by 31 regular contributors (retired rangers, trail runners, two search-and-rescue volunteers), and the county parks department's public closure feed, which is scattered across three websites. In the owner's words: "The product is freshness and judgment." AllTrails reviews are weeks or months old and written by strangers; parks sites publish closures but nothing about actual conditions. The owner wants this graded as a deliberately small business.

## Who pays, and for what
Committed local hikers who go out most weekends and plan around conditions pay $6/month, monthly only, no annual plan yet, for the Thursday-night conditions email. The owner estimates the buyer pool at 4,000-6,000 people in the metro who log 20+ hikes a year. That figure is an estimate built from the membership of the two largest local hiking Facebook groups (11k and 7k) cross-referenced with how many distinct people post trip reports. The owner says these buyers already spend $30-60/year on AllTrails Pro or Gaia.

## What exists today
- 39 consecutive weekly paid issues, zero missed weeks. In the flu week a contributor guest-wrote the issue (the one partial test of succession).
- 240 paying subscribers. 140 of them have been subscribed six months or longer.
- A free list of 1,900 subscribers who receive a monthly teaser issue.
- A 68% average open rate on paid issues.
- 31 contributors who file through a structured form. 12 are highly reliable weekly filers. None are paid; they get a free subscription and a named credit line.
- Two measured acquisition channels: a monthly mention in a local gear shop's newsletter (the shop gets a free sponsor slot in TrailNotes), and QR signs at 6 trailhead kiosks posted with written parks-department permission. The rest of growth is word of mouth.
- Tools: Substack, a weather API, form software, a domain.

## The operator
- Hours: 9-11 hours/week, logged for nine months. Two weekend scout hikes the owner would mostly do anyway (5-6 hours), compiling contributor reports and writing on Thursday (3 hours), admin (about 1 hour).
- Money available to spend: not stated. The owner states one ceiling for the 90 days: no spend above the current baseline of about $340/month.
- Skills: years of local hiking, nine months of running the paid product, an established contributor network.
- Hard constraints: bus factor of 1 (the owner personally produces every issue). Day job, other obligations, and location are not stated.

## Numbers you know
Owner-reported, sourced by the owner to Stripe, Substack analytics and the owner's own time log:
- Price: $6/month, monthly only.
- Subscribers: 240 paying, $1,440 MRR. Charging began nine months ago after four months free.
- Churn: 4.1% monthly averaged over the last six months (Stripe cohort data). Winter runs 5-6%, spring 2-3%. The owner's explanation: casual hikers pause in winter.
- Growth: net +30 subscribers/month averaged over the last three months. About 18/month attributed to the gear-shop mention, about 9/month to the QR signs, the remainder word of mouth.
- Costs, as the owner states them: Substack takes 10%; Stripe fees are about 2.9% + 30 cents; tools (weather API, form software, domain) $54/month; gas for scouting about $80/month. Total monthly cost "about $340 including payment fees at current MRR."
- Contribution margin, as the owner states it: "roughly 76% at current scale," with "no marginal cost per additional subscriber."
- Free-list conversion to paid: 1.7% of the free list per month, historically.

Estimates, not measurements: the 4,000-6,000 buyer pool, the AllTrails/Gaia spend of $30-60/year, and the owner's argument that AllTrails or a Facebook group could clone the format but have not because "the weekly legwork is the product." The owner labels that last point "an argument, not evidence."

## 90 days from now, done looks like
The owner's three measurable goals:
1. 330+ paying subscribers (from 240, at or above the measured +30/month rate) without raising spend above the current $340/month baseline.
2. An annual plan ($60/year, two months free) live, with at least 40 of the existing 240 converted, to blunt winter churn before it starts in November.
3. Bus-factor mitigation tested: two contributors trained, each solo-producing one full issue during the 90 days, with open-rate and churn impact measured against the owner's issues.

Out of scope for the 90 days: a second metro, sponsorships beyond the existing gear-shop swap, an app, and any paid acquisition. The question the plan answers, in the owner's words: "can a deliberately small, measured business harden its two known weaknesses (winter churn, bus factor) without breaking what works."

## Open unknowns
Intake would ask the owner these five questions in one batch. The owner is not available in this run, so each stays unanswered and is recorded here. Nothing below was answered by assumption.

1. **Which metro, and which state?** The 4,000-6,000 buyer pool and the local competitor picture cannot be checked against anything until the metro is named, and the state decides the tax and liability questions in question 5.
2. **How was "about $340/month, all-in" arrived at?** Is it a sum of itemized lines or a rough figure, and what were the actual Substack and Stripe fee amounts in the last full month?
3. **What can the owner commit beyond the 9-11 logged hours?** Is there a day job or other obligation competing for the 90 days, and how much money, if any, is available for one-off costs (contributor training, the annual-plan launch)?
4. **Which contributors would solo-produce an issue, and on what terms?** Has anyone among the 12 reliable filers agreed to it, and is there any written agreement covering the credit line, use of their reports, or their liability?
5. **What covers the liability and tax exposure?** Is there a disclaimer, insurance, or legal review for publishing "clear" or "closed" conditions a reader could be hurt relying on, and is sales tax on digital subscriptions handled? No jurisdiction is named, so any statement about local law here is an unverified local-law assumption and routes to the researcher once the state is known.

Also unknown, noticed in the dump and not asked:
- The basis for expecting at least 40 of 240 subscribers (about one in six) to take an annual plan that does not exist yet.
- Whether the annual plan carries the same platform and payment fees as the monthly plan.
- What happens to the newsletter if Substack changes its fee or rules. The owner's only stated mitigation is that the email list is exportable and his.
