# Business Plan: TrailNotes

Built from outputs/business-brief.md on 2026-09-29. Grades and revisions tracked via workflows/scorecard-*.md.

**Specimen disclosure:** TrailNotes is Backbrief's disclosed invented example business, not a real company. The plan uses the brief's figures as the owner's own statements. The unit-economics section was built by the builder with the unit-economics skill; one researcher pass retrieved the outside facts tagged below.

## Offer
Committed local hikers in one metro pay $6/month for TrailNotes, a Thursday-night email that reports, trail by trail, whether each trail is clear, muddy, snowed in, washed out or closed this week, with parking notes and one weekend pick, compiled from the owner's own weekly scouting, a 31-person contributor network and the county closure feed.

## Customer and market evidence
**Buyer.** Someone in the metro who logs 20+ hikes a year and plans around conditions. The owner estimates 4,000-6,000 such people from the membership of two local Facebook groups (11k and 7k) cross-referenced with distinct trip-report posters. That estimate is the owner's own and is **unverified**; the metro is unnamed, so no outside source can check it.

**Demand already paid for.** The strongest evidence is the business's own record, as the owner reports it from Stripe, Substack analytics and a time log: 240 paying subscribers, $1,440 MRR, 39 consecutive issues, 68% average paid open rate, 140 of the 240 subscribed six months or longer, 4.1% monthly churn. These are owner-reported records, not independently checked.

**Three tiers of evidence, kept apart.** (1) Owner-reported paid behavior: the subscriber, revenue, churn, open-rate and channel figures above. Measured by the owner from Stripe and Substack; not independently checked. (2) Verified outside facts: the AllTrails Plus and Gaia Premium prices below, retrieved from vendor pages on 2026-09-29. (3) Owner estimate, **unverified**: the 4,000-6,000 buyer pool. Tier 3 does not carry the 90-day goal; tier 1 does.

**What the pool estimate bears on.** The 90-day goal is 330 subscribers. At the measured 4.1% monthly churn (about 10 subscribers a month on 240), net +30/month means about 40 gross signups a month, so the goal needs about 120 gross signups in 90 days at the rate the owner already measures. That is 2.1% to 3.2% of the unserved pool if the owner's 4,000-6,000 is right, and 4.3% to 6.8% if the pool is half that size. The goal needs no new channel and does not depend on the pool being right. The long-run ceiling does: at constant gross signups of about 40/month and 4.1% churn, subscribers level off near 970 (adds equal churn), which is 16% to 24% of the owner's pool and 32% to 49% of half of it. Current penetration is 4.0% to 6.0% of the owner's pool (8% to 12% if the pool is half). The owner calls this "deliberately a small business"; the arithmetic agrees, and the pool figure would decide where the ceiling binds.

**Comparables (retrieved 2026-09-29, vendor pages, verified).** AllTrails Plus is $35.99/year (the owner calls it "Pro"; AllTrails now names it Plus). Gaia GPS Premium is $59.90/year. Both fall inside the owner's "$30-60/yr" claim. Neither page showed a monthly price, so any monthly figure is unverified. TrailNotes at $6/month is $72/year, higher than both; the $60 annual plan matches Gaia's price. Neither comparable publishes weekly, metro-level conditions, per the owner; the plan holds no outside check of that claim.

**What the evidence does not show, and how each gap gets checked.** Three gaps remain, and none can be closed from the brief.
1. *The tier 1 figures are the owner's word.* Unit 15 has the owner export the Stripe subscriber, MRR and cohort-churn records and the Substack open-rate report, and has the verifier compare each figure in the brief against them. A figure that matches is corroborated; one that does not is recorded with the difference.
2. *The pool has no method.* Once the owner names the metro, unit 13 counts the distinct people who posted a trip report in the two Facebook groups over a stated window. That count is a measured low bound for the pool. The owner's 6,000 stays an **unverified** upper bound until a second source exists, so the pool is stated as a range with one measured end.
3. *No local competitor search has been done.* Unit 13 lists every local source of trail conditions with its update frequency and price, and tests the freshness claim directly (method in unit 13). Unit 14 asks paying subscribers what they would use instead. A market-validation pass by the researcher is offered once, inside unit 13; the owner takes it or declines it when the plan is approved.

**Gate.** Unit 13 ends with a recorded owner decision before the annual-plan announcement (unit 4) goes out: if the measured low bound is under half of the owner's 4,000, or a free source publishes current conditions for most of the sampled trailheads, the owner reviews goals 1 and 2 first. The owner's answer to cloning ("the weekly legwork is the product") is labeled by the owner "an argument, not evidence." The plan keeps that label.

## Channels
Ranked by measured yield (owner-reported):
1. **Gear-shop newsletter mention:** about 18 signups/month. Cost: a free sponsor slot in TrailNotes (unpriced) and no cash.
2. **Trailhead QR signs at 6 kiosks:** about 9 signups/month. Written parks-department permission exists. Cost per sign not stated.
3. **Free-list conversion:** the 1,900-person free list converts at 1.7%/month historically; attribution overlaps with channels 1 and 2 (open item 4).
4. **Word of mouth:** the remainder of net +30/month. Cost: none stated.

Paid acquisition and sponsorships beyond the gear-shop swap are out of scope for the 90 days.

## Risks and mitigations
1. **Bus factor of 1.** The owner produces every issue; succession was tested once (flu week). Mitigation: units 5 to 7 (two contributors each solo-produce one issue; written on-call protocol).
2. **Winter churn (5-6% against 4.1% average).** Mitigation: annual plan live before November (units 3 and 4), measured in unit 9. The discount costs $7.15 net per subscriber-year (model section), accepted by the owner's own goal 2.
3. **Platform risk (Substack fee or rules change).** Mitigation: unit 11 verifies the list export works. No second platform is planned; the owner accepts the residual.
4. **Cost stack and spend ceiling do not reconcile.** Mitigation: units 1 and 2. Until they run, the plan's margin is shown on both totals.
5. **Liability for a wrong "clear" report, and sales tax on digital subscriptions.** The state is not named, so this is an **unverified local-law assumption**, not a mitigation. Unit 10 routes it to the researcher once the owner names the state. Nothing counts as covered until that retrieval is cited.
6. **Unpaid contributors leave.** 12 reliable filers get a free subscription and a credit line only. Mitigation: unit 5 asks the two selected contributors for a written yes; broader attrition is accepted by the owner and measured in unit 8 (filer count per week).
7. **Clone or free substitute (AllTrails, a Facebook group).** The owner's answer is "an argument, not evidence." Mitigation: none beyond the 39-week head start and the contributor network; the owner accepts the residual.
8. **Growth capped by the metro's committed-hiker population.** The owner grades this "deliberately a small business." Accepted; the penetration sensitivity is in the market section.

## Differentiation
The obvious alternatives are AllTrails or Gaia (verified $35.99/year and $59.90/year), a free local Facebook group, and doing nothing. TrailNotes is an add-on to the first two, not a replacement: the owner says buyers "already spend $30-60/yr" on them and still arrive at trailheads to a full lot or a washed-out trail.

**The concrete reason to pay, in three parts, each with its evidence and its limit:**
1. **Freshness and one accountable voice.** Reports are from this week, for one metro, from the owner's own scouting plus contributors, with parks closures compiled from three scattered sites. Evidence: owner-reported only (39 consecutive issues, 68% average open rate). Limit: no outside check that stale reviews are the alternative buyers actually face. Unit 13 tests it: for a sample of trailheads over consecutive weeks, it records whether TrailNotes and each local substitute carried a condition report dated in that same week.
2. **Source access a substitute would have to rebuild.** 31 contributors, 12 of them reliable weekly filers, including retired rangers, trail runners and two search-and-rescue volunteers, paid only in a free subscription and a credit line. Limit: unpaid volunteers can leave (risk 6), and a competitor could recruit the same people.
3. **Buyers choose it again every month.** The plan is monthly only, so each subscriber re-decides monthly with free and cheaper alternatives in reach. Evidence: 4.1% average monthly churn and 140 of 240 subscribed six months or longer (owner-reported). Limit: this shows retention, not that no substitute exists; unit 14 asks subscribers what they would use instead.

**Price against the comparables.** At $72/year on the monthly plan, TrailNotes costs about double AllTrails Plus and more than Gaia Premium; the $60 annual plan matches Gaia. The owner's justification, "$6/month is less than one wasted drive," is an argument, not evidence. Two owner-reported signals bear on price, and neither is a test of $72/year against a stranger: existing buyers stay at $6/month (4.1% monthly churn), and about 27 new buyers a month arrive through the gear-shop mention and the trailhead QR signs (18 + 9) and pay $6/month. Both are owner-reported, and unit 15 checks them against the Stripe records. The plan holds no evidence on whether a buyer who has not yet seen an issue would pay $72/year.

**Clone alarm (a trigger, not a moat).** The plan states the moat claim only as far as its evidence goes: nobody has cloned the format in 39 weeks, per the owner, and no outside search has confirmed it. To keep the clone risk from being merely accepted (risk 7), the day-90 review (unit 12) also reads two signals: (a) a free source found by the unit 13 audit that publishes current conditions for a large share of the trailheads TrailNotes covers, and (b) any month of paid churn above 6%, the top of the owner's stated 5-6% winter range. Either signal makes the owner decide within seven days whether to change coverage or price, inside the plan's out-of-scope limits (no paid acquisition, no second metro). The claim that no one else does this stays the owner's own, unchecked, until unit 13 reports.
