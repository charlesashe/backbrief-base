# Team Roles Template

Fill this in once, as a team, before more than one person starts running the product day to day. Revisit it whenever someone joins, leaves, or changes what they own. Ten minutes now answers a question that otherwise gets guessed at under pressure: who is supposed to be doing this?

## 1. Who drives which work

List every teammate who runs the product, and what they typically drive. Most teams find each person settles into one or two lanes rather than touching everything. Each row in this section is also a person the product's license covers or does not (see the product's own license file at the download root), so keeping the table current keeps that a maintained list instead of a guess.

## 2. Who runs verification before anything ships

Every finished piece of work gets checked in a fresh, separate pass before it goes out the door. Name the person (or people, on a rotation) who runs that check.

**Who runs the verifier before a release:** _______________

If this row is blank, that is the gap to close first. A team that skips this step is shipping unchecked work and calling it reviewed.

## 3. Who owns the weekly decision-log review

The project keeps a running log of the choices that were made and why. Someone should read it end to end on a regular cadence, not just add to it.

**Who owns the weekly review:** _______________

**Cadence:** _______________ (e.g. every Monday morning)

## 4. Who holds approval authority

This is the one row that does not get a list of names. A business plan gets pressure-tested before anyone acts on it, and someone has to give the final go-ahead before spending, publishing, or committing to it. That authority belongs to exactly one person on the team.

**The approving owner:** _______________

**This does not spread.** Whoever runs the day-to-day work, whoever drives the marketing or finance side, whoever manages the team calendar: none of that carries approval authority unless they are also the name written above. A teammate acting on a plan that was never actually approved by that one person is not authorized, no matter how far along the work is or how confident everyone feels about it. If the approving owner changes, update this row the same day, not after the fact.

## Quality check before you call this done

- [ ] Every teammate who runs the product has a row in section 1.
- [ ] Section 2's blank is filled in, or flagged as a real gap.
- [ ] Section 3 has both a name and a cadence.
- [ ] Section 4 names exactly one person, and everyone on the team has read that it does not spread.

## When to revisit

Update this template the moment a role changes hands, not at the next scheduled review. A stale row here is worse than an honest blank one, because a blank prompts a question and a stale answer does not.
