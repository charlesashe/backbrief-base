# Monetization path selection: pick one, then define it in four cells

Most one-person businesses lose their first year to a decision nobody made explicitly: which of
several plausible ways to make money they are actually running. Running three half-heartedly reads
as prudent diversification and behaves like abandoning each one before it compounds. This skill
forces the choice with a two-axis frame, then makes the chosen thing concrete enough to build or
sell.

It is a **selection and definition** procedure, not a build methodology. It stops where
construction starts. For the build, a plan with acceptance criteria first (the planner); for a business decision
proper, the CEO loop (`/intake`, `/business-plan`, `/grade`, `/approve`) is the route, and this
skill is planning support inside it.

## Step 1 - place the candidates on two axes

Draw two axes and put every path you are actually considering on them.

- **Vertical: leverage.** Does another customer cost you more hours? Coaching does - one more
  client is one more call. A product does not - one more signup is a row in a database. This is the
  axis that decides whether the business can outgrow your calendar.
- **Horizontal: time to first dollar.** How long until money arrives at all.

The frame's whole value is that it names the trade instead of hiding it. **Fast money sits
bottom-left and is bought with your hours. Scalable money sits top-right and is bought with time
before revenue.** There is no square that is fast and leveraged, and a plan that claims one is
usually mispricing the time.

The source's own map of twelve paths, as **ordinal reference, not forecast**:

| Roughly where it sits | Paths |
|---|---|
| Fast, low leverage (bottom-left) | 1:1 coaching, corporate training, AI strategy consulting, social media agency |
| Slow, low leverage (bottom-right) | AI automation agency, paid speaking, vibe-code agency, AEO agency |
| Slow, high leverage (top-right) | Paid courses, paid community, brand sponsorships, **your own product** |

The two slow rows are one x-band, not an ordering: several bottom-right paths sit later on her
chart than several top-right ones. What separates the rows is leverage, not time.

**Read those positions as ordering, never as numbers.** Her chart's vertical axis is labeled
"Leverage" but ticked in dollars from $0 to $10,000,000, which are two different quantities, and no
position on it is sourced to data. It is one operator's judgment, drawn on a whiteboard. Use it to
argue about relative placement; do not carry a tick label into a plan.

## Step 2 - choose exactly one, and commit for three to six months

- **One path.** Multiple income streams are not forbidden and early experimentation is encouraged,
  but the source's position - and the reason this is a selection skill - is that scaling past a
  certain point across several streams is materially harder than scaling one. Treat that as her
  claim and as a working default, not as a law.
- **An experiment is three to six months, not two weeks** - her window, stated at 06:16, and a
  recommendation rather than a measured finding. Anything shorter tests your patience rather than
  the path. A half-hearted quarter produces no signal and costs a quarter.
- **Choose the one you will still be doing in month five.** Consistency is what compounds, and
  enjoyment is the only durable input to consistency. This outranks picking the theoretically
  highest-leverage box you dread.
- **The decision is reversible and the skills transfer.** Speaking that plateaus funds a community;
  a course whose buyers never finish points at coaching. The point of choosing is to run one thing
  properly, not to be right forever.

## Step 3 - if the chosen path is your own product, define it before building

Fill four cells, **one sentence each**. A cell you cannot fill in one sentence is a cell you have
not decided.

| Cell | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **PATH** | What kind of business this is, in the vocabulary of step 1. |
| **OUTCOME** | The specific result the buyer gets, stated as a result and not as a feature. |
| **EXAMPLE BUYER** | One nameable kind of person, not a market segment. |
| **DISTRO** | Where these people will actually see it. |

Then the test that does the most work:

> **What is the ONE thing a user can walk away with in their first 90 seconds?**

If the honest answer needs onboarding, a tutorial, or "well, once they connect their account", the
product is not defined yet. Her own worked example fills the card as: vibe-coded product / one week
of content in ten minutes / content creators / TikTok plus YT, FB, IG, X.

**DISTRO is a cell you fill before building, not after.** A product defined without a distribution
answer is a build with a marketing problem scheduled for later, and later is when it is most
expensive. **Where the source names a platform, your own measurement outranks it.** Her "TikTok is
most generous with views" is a claim about her market and her account, and the platform that
distributes to non-followers in YOUR niche is an empirical question with a cheap answer: post the
same asset to two or three and read the reach. Pick DISTRO on what you measured, not on what a
creator with a different audience found.

## Step 4 - price the product path honestly before taking it

The product path is the highest-leverage box on the chart and **the longest to a first dollar of
all twelve**. Four things to say plainly to anyone choosing it:

1. **Recurring is not passive.** Subscription revenue arrives monthly and so do support tickets,
   bugs and maintenance. The leverage is real; the idleness is not.
2. **The first $1,000/month is the hard part.** The source's ladder - that 1k implies 10k implies
   100k - is her claim, unverified, and the useful half is the front of it: the gap from zero to
   the first thousand is where nearly everyone stalls, and stalling there is normal rather than
   diagnostic of a bad product.
3. **"Why is nobody buying this?" is usually a distribution question, not a product one.** Never
   diagnose a content problem from a distribution problem. A hook that only 171 people saw tells
   you nothing about the hook; rewriting it is the expensive way to avoid fixing the reach.
4. **Her own disqualifier, which cuts against her recommendation and is the most useful line in the
   source.** With no existing audience, no marketing background and no partnerships, take a faster
   path first, build the skills and the audience there, and come back to the product. This agrees
   with a companion skill outside this product, distilled independently from the same creator, which puts a
   software product last for the same reason. Two sources reaching that from different directions
   is the strongest thing in either.

**Enterprise-value talk stops here.** The source's acquisition figures - a $1M/year product
supporting a $5M-$20M sale price, and a claim that AI companies go for more than 20x revenue - are
unverified, and acquisition arithmetic is financial speculation. If a plan needs valuation, that is
analysis for the builder, loading the unit-economics skill under its analysis-not-advice framing, sourced to real comparables, not a
number carried out of a video.

## The gates this method arrives without

- **Pricing is the owner's decision, always** (escalation rule). This skill produces path options
  and a product definition. It never sets, quotes or publishes a price, and a price enters the
  decision log only as the owner's own recorded choice.
- **Distribution is an outward action.** Every post, DM, listing and launch in a DISTRO cell is
  prepared done-but-unsent and waits for the owner's GO.
- **Choosing a path for a business is business-decision work**, so it routes through the CEO loop
  rather than straight to execution work. Under the ceo-gate rule this skill is planning support; it
  unlocks nothing.
- **Nothing here is a forecast.** No income figure in the source is a projection for anyone, this
  skill promises none, and the chart is one operator's ordinal judgment. The only claim the method
  itself makes is that an explicit choice between paths beats an implicit one, and that a product
  definition which survives the 90-second test is likelier to survive contact with a buyer. Treat
  both as hypotheses your own first quarter exists to test.

## Composition

With no audience yet, build the audience before running this method (audience first, offer
second); the audience-building skill is not part of this product. `market-validation` takes the
filled four-cell card and pressure-tests it. `unit-economics` is what turns a chosen path into numbers that either close or do not.
`/grade` scores the resulting plan, and dimension 2 (market realism) is exactly where an unfilled
EXAMPLE BUYER cell shows up as a low score.
